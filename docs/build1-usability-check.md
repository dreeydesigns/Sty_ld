# Build 1, future human usability check

This script has not been run with a participant. Automated mocked checks do not establish intuitive usability or live persistence. Recruit and run a session only after separate approval of the environment and any data writes.

Use an unfamiliar participant, a phone at 360px or her own phone, and approved published or mocked content. Explain that preferences do not reserve an appointment and estimates are not final quotes. Do not coach her toward particular controls. Ask her to think aloud and say when she believes each task is finished.

1. Find a Look she likes using search or filters. Open it, learn about its creator, and return to where she was browsing. Observe whether she retains her place and understands creator identity and Demo labels.
2. Keep that Look for later, reverse the action, then keep it again. Leave the page and find it in Saved. Observe whether Save, Unsave and Undo are understood without help.
3. Start a Plan from the saved Look. Choose services, a preferred date and time, salon or mobile mode, and a general area. Observe whether she understands what is saved and what is merely a preference.
4. Find the estimated cost. Ask her to explain the listed subtotal, missing prices and unconfirmed charges in her own words. Observe whether she mistakes any figure for a confirmed quote or booking.
5. Return to edit one preference and one service, then revisit the estimate and reload. Ask her to explain what changed and what she expects to happen next. Observe whether she expects Compare or Request to be available.

Record each task's completion without assistance, time taken if measured, wrong turns, assistance, uncertainty and the participant's actual words. Ask which next action felt unclear and whether anything looked confirmed or paid. Record failures as findings rather than explaining them away.

Repeat critical tasks with keyboard navigation and an actual screen reader in a separately recorded accessibility session. DOM label and focus checks alone do not prove screen reader usability. Testing with reduced motion must include visual observation as well as the media preference.
