The gap STYLD is built to close

Trust is decided off-platform

A client asks a friend, then a WhatsApp group, then hopes. Nothing about that choice is recorded, so a bad experience teaches the market nothing.

The plan lives in someone's head

Braids, nails, makeup and skin are booked as separate conversations with separate prices and separate times. The whole look has no single owner.

Good work is hard to prove

A professional's skill shows in their chair and on their phone, not in a portfolio a client can compare or a platform can verify.

We describe the problem we are solving. We are not quoting market sizes, provider counts or retention figures we cannot show you in a database.

How a client moves through STYLD

  1. 01

    Discover

    Browse services and creator profiles, filter by what you actually need, and read listed information rather than a sales pitch.

  2. 02

    Compare

    Shortlist providers and put them side by side on the same facts: service, listed price, location and mode.

  3. 03

    Plan

    Build one beauty plan for the occasion. It is revision-controlled, so a change you make and a change someone else makes cannot overwrite each other.

  4. 04

    Agree

    Send a provider a request to discuss the plan. They accept or decline in the product, and the decision is stored. Live booking commitments and payments are still being connected.

Where professionals and salons fit

One identity, many workspaces

A person can be a client, a professional and a salon on a single account. Each workspace keeps its own profile, services and requests, so the identity does not have to be duplicated.

Work that can be reviewed

Creators publish looks to a feed that goes through a moderation queue before it is public. The queue decision is a server record, not a promise.

Applications, not open doors

Professional and salon access is granted through an application that an administrator reviews. Retail shops and delivery fleets are deliberately closed while the marketplace side is proven.

What works today, and what is still being tested

Working in the private beta

  • Email one-time-passcode sign-in, with sessions that are independent per device.
  • Discovery of services and public creator profiles.
  • The beauty plan, with revision control and optimistic locking.
  • Shortlist and side-by-side compare.
  • Six beauty worlds with three intensity levels.
  • Requests to discuss a plan, accepted or declined by the provider on record.
  • Community publishing behind a moderation queue, and private saves, blocks and reports.
  • Restricted media delivery: a pending image is only reachable by its owner or an MFA-verified reviewer, through a short signed window.
  • Administrator routes behind MFA, with an audit trail of decisions.

Not enabled yet

  • Live booking commitments. The product will not accept a booking until real pricing and availability are connected; it says so on screen instead of pretending.
  • Payments. No gateway is live and no card or M-Pesa charge is taken.
  • Reviews. Nothing is shown as a review until a completed service is proven by a server record.
  • Verified-provider badges. Not issued, so not displayed.
  • Public image uploads. Held pending moderation.
  • Shop and Delivery roles. Registration of interest only.
  • Open sign-up. The beta is invite-only while the security gates are being closed.

This split is read from the shipped build, not from a marketing plan. We would rather show an investor what is unfinished than a number that is not real.

Trust is a build decision

Server-side authorisation

Roles and workspaces are resolved from the session on the server. A request that names someone else's account gets a refusal, not that account's data.

Fail closed

Where a gate is not configured, the route refuses. Uploads, reviews, payments and booking commitments are unavailable by default rather than optimistic.

Honest language

The product never tells a customer that something is confirmed, secured or verified unless the record proves it. That rule applies to this page too.

Why Nairobi, and why now

Nairobi's beauty economy already runs on relationships: the braider a cousin recommends, the nail technician who knows your hairline, the salon that answers at 9pm. That knowledge is real, and it is also completely offline.

We are starting in one city, with one category we can do properly, and with the records we would need to prove quality later. A marketplace earns trust city by city, and a beauty platform earns it appointment by appointment. Once the plan, the agreement and the provider record hold in Nairobi, the same structure carries to the next city without inventing a new product.
